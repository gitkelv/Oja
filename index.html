How it works

Three steps. Nobody signs up, and no dealer joins anything.

  1. 1

    Say what you need

    The material, how much of it, and where it is going. Delivery is usually what decides the real cost, so where it is going matters as much as how much.

  2. 2

    Approve each dealer

    Before a dealer is contacted you see exactly which site is about to be asked, and you allow or decline it. Nothing is contacted without you.

  3. 3

    Compare what came back

    Each answer lands on its own card with its own date. Where dealers disagree, you see the gap and who is on each side of it.

Know the limits

Better you hear it here than find out later.

These dealers are not real businesses.

They are published by this project to demonstrate the protocol, and they trade in an invented town. Nothing here can be bought.

The prices are representative, not live.

They come from dated published Nigerian market reporting, cited on each dealer's own page. A real deployment would carry each dealer's own figures.

It takes a couple of minutes.

Dealers are asked one at a time, and each is a separate website running its own agent inside your browser. That wait is the cost of asking real parties separately instead of reading one list.

It only knows what a dealer publishes.

If a dealer does not state its stock, Ọjà says so rather than guessing. An empty field means nobody was told, not that the answer is zero.

There is no single price, and there should not be.

Published Nigerian cement prices already contradict each other by nearly double. Averaging them turns a disagreement you could have acted on into a confident number that is wrong.
